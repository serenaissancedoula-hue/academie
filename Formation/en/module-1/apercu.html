Reminder of scope. The doula does not palpate the uterus, does not inspect pads or the perineum, and does not measure blood loss. She gathers what the mother describes, helps her observe herself following the instructions given by her midwife or care team, and refers. All the semiology presented here is for spotting concerns, never for diagnosis.

1.1.1 Uterine involution

Uterine involution is the return of the pregnant uterus, about 1,000 g immediately after delivery, to a pelvic organ of 60 to 100 g by about the sixth week. This active process involves muscle contraction, enzymatic breakdown of tissue and complete regeneration of the endometrium.

Starting point: the uterus at delivery

During pregnancy, the myometrium adapts through hypertrophy (an increase in cell size) far more than through hyperplasia. Each myocyte lengthens to as much as ten times its original length. The uterine blood supply then receives about 10 to 15% of cardiac output, close to 500 to 700 mL of blood per minute at term.

After the placenta is expelled, the placental site leaves a wound about 8 to 10 cm in diameter, crossed by widely open spiral arteries. Without a mechanism to stop it, this wound would bleed massively within minutes. This is where the living ligature comes in.
